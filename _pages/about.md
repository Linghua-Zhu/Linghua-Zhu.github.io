Assistant Professor of Computer Science, Colorado School of Mines, from January 2027.
Currently Research Scientist, Department of Chemistry, University of Washington.

My long-term goal is to design quantum computation from the structure of the problem it is
meant to solve, and to co-design the layers that this structure touches, including the
encoding, the algorithm, the error correction, and the hardware underneath. These layers are
currently built in isolation, each treating the one below it as uniform and fixed. That
assumption is where most of the cost comes from, and structure is what makes it unnecessary.

Before Washington I was a postdoctoral researcher at Virginia Tech, where I worked on adaptive
variational algorithms for chemistry and optimization. I hold a PhD in Physics from the New
Jersey Institute of Technology and Rutgers University, and a BS in Physics from the University
of Science and Technology of China.

Much of my work sits between computer science and electronic structure theory. Several years
inside a quantum chemistry group have shaped how I read a quantum algorithm. The question is
rarely whether a circuit runs, and almost always whether the number it produces means anything
to a chemist.

## Quantum MOTIF Lab

A motif is the smallest unit that still carries meaning. In chemistry it is a recurring
structural fragment, in music a short phrase that an entire movement is built from. We think
quantum computation should be designed the same way, from the structure a problem already has,
carried down through every layer to the machine.
